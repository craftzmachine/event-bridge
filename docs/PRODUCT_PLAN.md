# Event Bridge — product plan

How to turn Event Bridge from Parinay Weddings' in-house workspace into a product other wedding-planning companies pay for: what to sell, at what tiers, and what has to be built first.

> **Status (27 November 2026):** everything listed under "Available today" exists in the app. Anything marked **Roadmap** is not built yet and must not be promised to customers as available.

---

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** the operating system for Indian wedding planners — from the first enquiry to the last expense, in one place.

**Who it's for**
- **Solo planners and small studios** (1–3 people) who quote from Excel and WhatsApp today.
- **Growing planning companies** (4–15 people) juggling several weddings a month, staff advances and vendor payments.
- **Agencies and venue-led planners** (multiple branches or cities) who need consistent branding, approvals and reporting.

**The problem it solves**
- Proposals take hours in Excel and look different every time.
- Nobody knows which enquiry needs a call today.
- Checklists, coordination sheets and payments live in separate notebooks and chats.
- Staff spend company money on site, and the expense trail is a pile of GPay screenshots.

**Why Event Bridge wins**
| Strength | What it means for a planner |
|---|---|
| Proposal Studio | A branded, consistent quotation in minutes, as PDF, Excel or a private web page |
| Indian money rules built in | ₹ with Indian digit grouping, GST as a reference figure, co-ordination %, round-off, 70/30 payment plans |
| Multi-function weddings | Haldi, Sangeet, Muhurtham and Reception on one proposal, each with its own subtotal |
| Client portal | The family approves, ticks their to-dos and answers questionnaires on one private page |
| Expense control | Proof for every rupee, duplicate-UPI blocking, staff advances and a handover reviewed by someone else |
| Costs stay private | Internal costs and margin never reach anything the client sees |
| WhatsApp-first | One tap opens a ready message to the client or vendor |

## 3. What must change before selling (honest gaps)

The app was built for one company. These are the gaps between today and a product:

| Today | Why it blocks selling |
|---|---|
| One company's branding, catalogue and venues in `public/config/*.json` | Every customer would need their own deployment and hand-edited JSON |
| Accounts are a fixed list compiled into the site | No sign-up, no invites, no password reset; the browser check is not real security |
| Proposals, CRM, tasks and vendors live in one browser (localStorage); expenses in IndexedDB | A team can't share work; clearing the browser loses data; JSON backup is the only sync |
| Only Visualize / portal sharing has a server | Everything else needs a real backend |
| Venue directory covers Thiruvananthapuram only | Little value outside Kerala |
| Hosted on Vercel Hobby | Vercel's terms limit Hobby to non-commercial use; a paid product needs Pro or another host |
| No billing, terms of service or privacy policy | Can't take payments or meet India's DPDP Act |

## 4. Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Sellable (must ship before the first paid customer) · ~3–4 months

1. **Workspaces (multi-tenancy).** Every record belongs to a company workspace. The company's brand, catalogue, checklists, questionnaires, packages and venues load from the workspace instead of `public/config`. Keep the current validators (`src/config/validate.ts`) as the server-side checks.
2. **Real accounts.** Server sign-in for everyone (the existing `/api/staff/*` path and the `SERVER_SIGNIN` switch are the starting point), email invites, password reset, optional Google sign-in, and sessions checked on the server.
3. **Roles.** Owner, manager, planner, accounts. Costs and margin visible to owner/manager only; advances managed by accounts (today's `advanceManagers` rule, generalised).
4. **Cloud database and sync.** Move proposals, leads, tasks, vendors, questionnaires and expenses to a hosted database (for example Postgres via Supabase or Neon) with file storage for proofs. One-time **import of existing JSON backups** so Parinay and early users keep their history.
5. **Settings screens.** *Started 27 November 2026:* the Settings page (`#/settings`) edits company details, logo, GST %, co-ordination %, terms, categories and elements, saved on the device with export/import, plus a "Set up Event Bridge" checklist on the Dashboard. Still to do: packages and checklists in the app, and storing settings per workspace on the server.
6. **Sign-up and billing.** Self-serve sign-up, 14-day trial, Razorpay subscriptions (UPI autopay, cards), GST invoices for the subscription, plan limits enforced on the server.
7. **Trust basics.** Terms of service, privacy policy, DPDP Act consent and data-export / delete-my-workspace, daily backups, uptime monitoring, a status page.
8. **Hosting.** Move to a commercial plan (Vercel Pro or equivalent) with the database in an Indian region.

### Phase 1 — Retention · ~2–3 months after launch
- **Works offline, syncs later** — the current local-first design becomes a cache, so venues with no signal still work.
- **Installable phone app (PWA)** with camera capture for expense proofs and a share target for GPay screenshots.
- **Reminders:** follow-ups, overdue checklist tasks and payment milestones by email and WhatsApp Business API.
- **Audit log** of who changed what (proposals, payments, expenses).
- **Onboarding:** reuse Wedding Quest as the first-run guide; sample wedding in every new workspace.
- **Template gallery:** starter catalogues by region and ceremony (Kerala Hindu, Christian, Muslim; North Indian; destination), checklists and questionnaires a new customer can copy.
- **Proposal versions** and a side-by-side "what changed" for the family.

### Phase 2 — Growth · 6+ months
- **Venue directories by city** (Kochi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Goa, Jaipur, Udaipur…), researched and sourced the same way as today's.
- **Expense import:** read amount, date and UPI ID from GPay screenshots (in-browser OCR), parse bank SMS, match bank statements.
- **GST tax invoices** to clients, and **accounting export** to Tally and Zoho Books.
- **Reports:** conversion by lead source, average wedding value, margin by category, vendor spend.
- **White-label:** the customer's own domain for client links and portal, branded emails.
- **Branches:** several teams under one company with roll-up dashboards.
- **Vendor network:** shared vendor profiles and availability requests.
- **Multi-currency** for destination and NRI weddings.
- **Integrations:** Google Calendar, Google Drive / Sheets export, Zapier-style webhooks.

## 5. Risks and what not to promise

- **Screenshots can't be fully blocked** in a browser. Visualize and the client viewer discourage and watermark them; say "discourages copying", never "prevents screenshots".
- **Until Phase 0 ships, data lives on one device.** Don't sell to multi-person teams before cloud sync, or make backups part of onboarding.
- **Venue facts** must stay sourced; tell customers to confirm capacities with the venue before quoting.
- **WhatsApp buttons only open a message.** Automatic sending needs the WhatsApp Business API (Phase 1) and its per-message costs.
- **Parinay branding** must be removed from the generic product (landing page photos, crest, sample terms). Parinay becomes customer #1, not the brand of the product.

## 6. Go-to-market

1. **Pilot (month 1–2 after Phase 0):** 3–5 planners in Kerala, free for 3 months in exchange for weekly feedback and a testimonial. Measure: proposals sent per week, time to first proposal, weekly active users.
2. **Launch offer:** founding-customer price locked for 2 years (for example 30% off Professional).
3. **Channels:** planner associations and WhatsApp communities, Instagram demos (a 60-second "proposal in 3 minutes" reel), venue partnerships (venues recommend the platform to their planners), referral credit of one free month.
4. **Onboarding checklist for each customer:** company details and logo → catalogue import from their Excel → invite team → first proposal → first client share → first expense.
5. **Support model:** help centre (`docs/user-guide/`), in-app Help, a WhatsApp support line during business hours, monthly webinar for new customers.

### Success measures
- Trial → paid conversion ≥ 25%.
- Monthly churn ≤ 3%.
- Median time from sign-up to first sent proposal ≤ 1 day.
- ≥ 60% of paying teams using at least four apps (Studio, Events, CRM, Expenses).
